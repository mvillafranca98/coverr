01

The problem, stated once

A band posts a flyer with a phone number and a bank account. Buyers transfer, screenshot the confirmation, and send it over WhatsApp. Someone types every name into a spreadsheet by hand. The venue gets asked questions that aren’t its job to answer.

On the night, staff check printed lists by name, take cash and transfers at the door, photograph screens, and tally headcount on paper. Wristbands, screenshots and the paper count never agree, so the band recounts and averages.

What the last event actually looked like

ChannelPeopleMoneyFailure mode
Presale92L18,400Hand-typed from WhatsApp, one row at a time
Door — transfer84L21,000Queue stalls while staff read screenshots
Door — cash53L13,250Tallied on paper, never matches
Comps4L0Outside the numbering, invisible to the count

Two observations from that data shaped this spec. 39% of door sales were cash — so a transfer-only product solves the easy half and leaves the hard half broken. And 84 × 250 = 21,000 exactly, 53 × 250 = 13,250 exactly — one of those figures was divided out of the other, so the reconciliation was never an independent check at all.

02

Decisions already settled

These are closed. Recorded here so they don’t get relitigated mid-build.

DecisionWhy
Money never passes through the platformEach band collects into its own account. Keeps this a SaaS tool rather than a money transmitter, and removes the payout obligation entirely.
No card processingNo PixelPay, Stripe or PayPal. Bank transfer only, which is what buyers already use.
QR issues immediately, alwaysThe buyer is never held in a pending state. Verification is internal bookkeeping, not a gate on the customer.
Verification is deterministic, not AI judgmentEvery flag is a checkable fact with a reason you can say out loud. No model ever calls a real buyer a forger.
No email forwarding setupBand admins won’t configure it. BAC’s own alert email is also useless for matching — no sender name, no reference.
No notificar a addressTyping an unfamiliar email into a bank transfer form feels like a scam to the person doing it. Abandonment risk outweighs the data.
No per-account ownership trackingThe band owns its own arithmetic. A per-account subtotal on the dashboard is the whole feature.
Cash is first-classA staff cash-sale button, not an afterthought. It’s 39% of the door.
Comps are first-classIssued a QR, checked in, counted in attendance, zero revenue.
Wristbands stayThird independent count, and the only thing that handles re-entry — a QR burns on first scan.
Email delivery, not WhatsAppAvoids leaving a dead one-message thread in every buyer’s inbox.

04

Buyer flow

One page, one scroll, no account. The link goes in the flyer, the bio, and the venue’s repost — which is what removes the venue from the conversation.

  1. Event pageBand, date, doors, venue, music, price. Presale price shown with remaining count if capped.
  2. QuantityPicker with running total. 4 × L200 = L800.
  3. Bank picker — “¿Con qué banco vas a pagar?”BAC / Ficohsa / Atlántida / Occidente / Banpaís / Otro. Selecting one shows only that bank’s account, large, tap-to-copy, labelled mismo banco — sin comisión. “Otro” shows the default account and an honest warning that their bank will charge roughly L40 in ACH fees.
  4. Optional detail codeA second tap-to-copy chip, JGL-4827, labelled opcional — nos ayuda a confirmarte más rápido. Never required, never validated, never blocks the QR. Two of three sample confirmations already had something written in that field unprompted.
  5. Upload the confirmationCamera or file. Accepts image or PDF. Uploads and extracts while the buyer keeps typing.
  6. Payer detailsName, email, phone.
  7. Attendee namesOne row per ticket, each with an optional note. Real examples from the current spreadsheet: “me dicen Mando”, “son dos personas con el mismo nombre”.
  8. ConsentRefund policy shown before purchase. Band picks none or 24h. Text is stored on the order, so a later policy change never applies retroactively.
  9. QR codes on screen, immediatelyOne per attendee, named, with a download button. Email follows with the same codes and the band’s thank-you note.

Three ways to get your ticket back

Email deliverability is the largest silent failure in the whole system. If the QR email lands in spam, everything breaks at the door. Three independent layers:

  1. On screen at purchase — the primary. Email is backup, not delivery.
  2. /recuperar — enter an email, get the tickets resent. Always returns the same message whether or not the address is registered, so nobody can probe who bought.
  3. Staff name search — the last resort, at the door, in the admin app.

Door flow

Scanning

ResultScreenStaff does
VerdeName, ticket 2 of 4, order codeWristband, let them in
ÁmbarSame, plus the reason: “faltan L50”, “referencia repetida”, “comprobante ilegible”Ask to see the confirmation, resolve or wave through
RojoAlready checked in at 21:04 by MarceloDuplicate scan — check the wristband

Amber must always name its reason on screen. Staff resolving a flag in five seconds is the entire point; staff phoning the admin is the failure.

Selling at the door

  • Cash sale — quantity, tap, done. Increments the count, creates ticket records, needs no QR. The wristband is the token.
  • Walk-up transfer — a printed QR poster at the entrance points at the same buyer flow at box-office price. They self-serve in the queue instead of holding it up.
  • Wristband counter — admin enters the starting count; the app shows what remaining should be at any moment.

Offline

The scanner caches the full attendee list on load and validates locally. Scans queue and sync on reconnect. With two devices offline simultaneously, a cross-device double scan is flagged at sync, not prevented — trying to prevent it needs connectivity the venue doesn’t have. The wristband is the real anti-passback control, which is exactly why it stays.

Band home and history

Section 6 is one event, live. This is what the band lands on when it logs in — every event it has ever run, and it stays open whether or not the next gig has been paid for. It is also the only part of the product a band would open on a Tuesday with no show booked.

No-show rate is the one that only exists because the platform does. Nothing in the current process compares who paid against who walked in — the printed list gets ticked and thrown away. After two events this becomes a real number, and it changes how many presale tickets are worth selling against a fixed capacity.

Costs, compared

The existing expense labels are already consistent across events — Sonido, Tarima, IG Boost, Comida, Taquilleros, Fotos, Decoración. That consistency is what makes “did we spend less this time” answerable, and it is fragile: one sonido or sonido y luces and the series breaks.

So Expense gains a normalised category alongside its free-text label. The label stays human (“Cristy — decoración tarima”); the category is what trends. Past labels autocomplete when entering a new expense.

Fans

Buyers are matched across events on normalised email and phone. This yields three things no spreadsheet can:

  • Unique people reached across every gig — the band’s actual audience size, as opposed to the sum of attendances
  • Returning share — what fraction of tonight’s room has been before
  • Regulars — anyone at three or more shows. That list is the band’s core following, with contactable emails, and it is worth more than any other output of this product

Comps are included in the fan view and excluded from every revenue figure.

What’s free and what’s gated

CapabilityAccess
Every past event’s dashboard, orders and settlementFree forever
History, trends, cost comparison, fansFree forever
CSV export of anything already unlockedFree forever
Running the next event end to end — presale, QRs, check-in, cash sales, live dashboardFree
Settlement report and export for that new eventUnlock fee

A band never loses access to anything it already has, and can run an entire gig before deciding to pay. The unlock fires at the moment of maximum value: the show is over, the money is in the account, and four people are waiting to be told their number.

This is a soft paywall, deliberately

A band could read gross off the live dashboard and do the splits by hand. The defence isn’t technical — it’s that the settlement report is the tedious part (expenses, advances, per-member payouts, the export), and L1,200 against L43,000 with bandmates waiting is not a fight anyone picks. Locking the live dashboard instead would break the product on the night, which is far worse than losing the occasional fee.

08

Verification pipeline

Runs on upload, in the background, while the buyer finishes typing. It never blocks the QR.

  1. Store and hashSHA-256 for exact duplicates, perceptual hash for re-crops and re-screenshots.
  2. Classifyapp_notification · web_result · alert_email · pdf_export · unknown. Unknown routes to manual review — it never guesses.
  3. ExtractPDF with a text layer → pdfminer, deterministic and free. Image → one vision call returning a strict JSON schema. Extraction is by label, never by position: the three samples render at three different widths and one is cropped on both edges.
  4. CheckThe five rules below, all deterministic.
  5. Record and notifySet verification_status, raise flags, email the admin if anything is critical.

sender_account  000000002          (web_result only)

One idea tested and discarded

Reference numbers looked sequential across two samples, which would have given a free forgery check. A third sample killed it — 30/07 20:20 → 412494357 is higher than 31/07 11:25 → 412420296. Not monotonic across days. Do not build range-interpolation checks on reference numbers.

Checks

CodeSeverityFires whenWhat you tell the buyer
REF_DUPLICATECriticalReference already used on another order, any event“Esa referencia ya se usó en otra compra”
DEST_MISMATCHCriticalDestination account isn’t one of the band’s“La transferencia no llegó a nuestra cuenta”
HASH_DUPLICATECriticalSame image already uploaded“Ese comprobante ya lo recibimos”
AMOUNT_SHORTWarningAmount < expected“Faltan L50”
AMOUNT_OVERWarningAmount > expectedUsually paying for someone else — admin confirms
DATE_STALEWarningTransaction predates the event going liveRecycled from an older transfer
UNREADABLEInfoExtraction failed or confidence lowNothing — manual review, buyer unaffected
NAME_MISMATCHInfoSender name doesn’t match payer nameNothing. Paying for friends is normal — never surface this

NAME_MISMATCH must never reach the buyer or the door. The baseline event has multi-ticket purchases throughout — one person paying for four is the common case, not the suspicious one. It exists only as a weak signal in the admin view.

Retention

Proof files auto-delete 30 days after the event. Sample web_result exposes a third party’s full account number and personal email, so this is real PII, not just an image. Encrypted at rest, admin-only access, deletion logged.

Notes on the model

  • PriceRule as a list, not an enum. “Presale until it sells out or until Friday, then box office” is already two rules with trigger conditions. Modelled this way, tiers later are configuration rather than a rewrite.
  • QR lives on Attendee, not Order. A group buying four arrives separately; each person needs their own code.
  • Comps are an Order with channel = comp and expected_amount = 0. They check in, they count toward attendance, they contribute nothing to revenue. The current spreadsheet keeps them outside the numbering entirely, which is one reason the headcount never matched.
  • A refund voids the QR and writes a negative line, so the settlement stays true without deleting history.
  • Contact is derived, not entered. Built by normalising payer email and phone across orders. It powers the fan view and lets a band see that someone has been to five shows without ever asking them to sign up.
  • Expense.category is what trends; label stays human. Free-text labels alone can’t be compared across events — one inconsistent spelling breaks the series.
  • bank_selected is worth storing even though buyers can ignore it — it tells you which account’s statement an order should appear in when you eventually import one.

Settlement

Restructured from the spreadsheet, which subtracts every cost from the presale bucket. That produces the right total only while all splits are equal — add one session musician at 10% and it stops being explainable to the people it’s paying.

Gross     = presale + door_transfer + door_cash      comps contribute 0
Costs     = ∑ expenses
Net       = Gross − Costs
Share[i]  = Net × pct[i]
Payout[i] = Share[i] − advances[i]

  • Percentages must total exactly 100 — the members table is saved whole and refuses anything else. A remainder the band holds back for the fund is a row of its own, Fondo de la banda, so every lempira of the net goes to a name. (Until 2026-11-04 this warned and never blocked.)
  • Advances are dated and carry across events, so an advance survives a show that underperforms.
  • Ticket counts come from records, never a typed cell. The current sheet hardcodes 92; add a presale row and revenue silently stops updating.
11

Pricing

The settlement report is the paywall. Presale, QR delivery, check-in, cash sales and the live dashboard are all free every time. The band pays to unlock the final settlement and export — after the event, once the tool has visibly done its job and there’s money in the account.

That timing also makes size-based pricing free to implement: the exact ticket count is already known when the charge fires, so the band never picks a plan and never negotiates.

Everything a band has already paid for stays open permanently, and the band home in section 7 — history, trends, cost comparison, fans — is never gated. The fee is per gig, not a subscription, and it buys the settlement for that gig.

Tickets soldFeeReasoning
First event, any size, held by 31 January 2027FreeFull features including the report. Nobody in this market pre-pays for unproven software from an unknown developer.
1 – 60L500A 40-person bar gig grosses ~L4,000, so this is 12.5% of it.
61 – 150L1,000
151 – 300L1,500The baseline event lands here — 2.8% of gross, 3.5% of net.
301 – 500L2,000For the bands this is built for, 300 to 500 people is a big night.
501 +L2,000 + L4 per ticket over 500A 1,000-person show pays L4,000, a 2,000-person one L8,000 — about 2% of gross at L200 a ticket. Processing a ticket costs Coverr roughly L0.30–L1 (one receipt read per order, plus an email), so this pays for itself several times over.
  • Large shows priced by size since 4 November 2026. The top bracket used to be L2,000 flat for any size from 301 up; it now stops at 500, and each ticket past that adds L4. Reports already unlocked keep their price.
  • Raised on 30 October 2026 from L400–L1,800 to L500–L2,000. An event already unlocked keeps the price it was unlocked at.
  • The totals show before the unlock. Decided on 3 November 2026: a locked report still shows gross, costs and net, so the band sees how the night went; the fee buys the per-member split, with advances already deducted. Nothing per member reaches the page until it is unlocked.
  • “Primer toque gratis” is a launch offer, limited on 3 November 2026. A band’s first event is free only if it is held on or before 31 January 2027, Honduras time — judged by when the show happens, not when it was created, so a March gig set up in December does not stretch the offer. A first event held later is charged by its bracket. The landing page drops its free tier when the offer ends; the date lives in one place, lib/settlement.ts.
  • Comps don’t count toward the bracket. They’re free tickets; don’t charge for them.
  • No subscription, no seat pricing, no expiry. A band that plays twice a year pays twice a year and keeps its history in between.
  • Sell to bands and event planners, not venues. The band holds the bank account and the pain; the venue holds neither. Bands also carry the tool from venue to venue, so venues see it repeatedly without being sold to.
  • Runtime cost is roughly $2 per event — about $0.70 in vision extraction across ~230 uploads, plus email. Margin holds even at the bottom bracket.
  • The fee is collected by bank transfer, on the same rail the product exists to fix.

13

Not in v1

Named here so they stay out of the build rather than creeping in.

  • Ticket tiers, VIP, early bird, assigned seating
  • Card payments of any kind
  • Bank statement import — pinned until the export format is known
  • WhatsApp delivery
